How does brainiac learn to think for itself?

brainiac is a small computer brain that learns from text, the way a child learns from stories. Here is the plan to help it grow its own logic, and where it stands today.

Updated 2026-11-26 21:41 PST

The big idea

brainiac grows its own sentence and logic structures. Nobody types the skills in. The code gives it only brain parts: cells, links that get stronger with use, a surprise signal, a reward, replay, sleep, and a place to save. Then it reads stories. Reading makes new cells and new links. One brain file is never wiped; it keeps growing.

A. Own logic structures

Means: brainiac builds its own kinds of thinking, not ones we code.

How: a ladder of 6 rungs: groups, compare, order and cause, not/all/some, if/then, question shapes.

B. Forming sentences

Means: brainiac says more than one word, and says it well.

How: four steps. (1) Reading grows the brain (KB-3712, done). (2) Stories feed its piece store (KB-3720). (3) It learns how sentences start (KB-3726). (4) It leans toward topics that paid off (KB-3728).

C. Story elements

Means: a retold story names who, where, and what happened, in order.

How: the order-and-cause rung plus a who/what/where rung. A new check (KB-3798): retell or continue a story naming a character, a place, and 2 events in order.

Now: waiting. The check is written down, not run.

D. Asking questions, and how to ask

Means: brainiac notices what it does not know and asks, in a good question.

How: it asks itself first (KB-3738); that counts only if the answer makes a later answer better. Then it asks you (KB-3740). It learns question openers with the sentence steps. A new check (KB-3800): well-formed question, right question word for the gap.

Inside brainiac itself, no separate copy. Each step is tried on a copy of the brain, behind a switch that is off by default.

1. The dream

Read it lots of storybooks. Over time it should start to wonder, and ask “why?” by itself.

2. The learning loop

One circle, over and over: read, keep the pieces, notice what is missing, ask, learn the answer, and tidy up while resting.
